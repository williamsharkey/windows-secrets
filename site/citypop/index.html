    CITYPOP1993 is a theme and eleven variations for a quiet night in a harbour town. It starts in the evening rain and ends at first light. Put it on after the club, or when friends are still on the couch and nobody wants to go home yet.

    The theme is eight bars long. A pick-up of four notes rises to the third and then leaps to the sixth, over Fmaj7, Em7, A7, Dm9 and G13. Every track after it is a variation. Each one moves the theme to another key, tempo or mode, puts it in waltz time or turns it upside down. Variation VII is the theme inverted, and it is the slow heart of the record. One variation quotes, once and briefly, the first bar of Paganini's Caprice No. 24 (1817, public domain).

    Every note was played by SNDWAVE1993. That is sixteen real Windows 3.11 Sound Recorders in DOSBox Pure, one Windows for each part, driven by MIDI and rendered offline. The mixer only sets levels and pans and adds a shared echo and room. The electric pianos and basses were recorded from the publisher's own instruments into the album's sound library, the Rainy Loft Collection, next to rain, a city at night, a far train and an espresso machine. Those field recordings are CC0.

    The variations

    • Theme in E-flat, plainly, with brushes. It ends on the IV: the evening is not over.
    • I The motto is traded between vibraphone and suitcase piano, once upside down. The flute plays an eighth late.
    • II Every long note is pushed a sixteenth early. Slap bass, brass, a step up a semitone.
    • III Bossa nova, with the theme in augmentation: each note twice as long.
    • IV The minor. A worn tape piano. The phone rings twice.
    • V Waltz time: each bar of the theme becomes two.
    • VI A minor, a train groove, and one bar of Paganini that turns into the theme's pick-up.
    • VII The theme inverted and re-harmonised, slow, for piano, strings and choir.
    • VIII Diminution: the theme twice as fast, over four on the floor.
    • IX Grain clouds made from steam and rain. The theme crosses once, very slowly.
    • X Tritone substitutions, trading fours, last call.
    • Finale The variations come back around the theme. Into F, ending on the I, at first light.
