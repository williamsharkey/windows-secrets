BASILISK SYNTH, the Windows XP edition

Final release 1.3.0; development continues on BASILISK 16.

BASILISK SYNTH plays your notes on sixteen real Windows XP Sound Recorders (sndrec32.exe) running inside the plugin. It takes the same DNA as BASILISK 16. BASILISK 16 is the instrument going forward: lower latency, less CPU, a much smaller download, and it runs in the browser.

How a note is played

Every DNA step is one of Sound Recorder's own menu commands, run by the real sndrec32.exe on its own buffer. The Windows sounds are XP's 35 files in C:\WINDOWS\Media, and the Windows 3.x recordings (since 1.2.1), so one name means the same file as in BASILISK 16.

A small DLL of ours is loaded into each sndrec32 process. When a note comes, it plays that recorder's own saved sound (its buffer, with its effects): it streams it to a sound-card handle opened once at 48 kHz, and applies the note's pitch itself, as a 16.16 fixed-point step through the sound, with linear interpolation in fixed point (integer arithmetic only: floating point is slow in the emulator), and the velocity, level, pan and envelope, on the samples, inside XP.

ours MIDI -> plugin: voice allocator, pitch, pan, envelope ours synth.exe (in Windows): signals recorder n XP sndrec32.exe: its own saved sound, with its effects ours srhook.dll (inside sndrec32): pitch step, velocity, level, pan, envelope -> card at 48 kHz XP XP's mixer -> AC97 sound card (in QEMU) -> a pipe to the plugin ours plugin: fixed latency, resampled to your session's rate, Output Gain -> DAW

The emulator is QEMU. The plugin never makes a sound of its own.

Presets

Each preset plays a short phrase of the kind it is made for, rendered through the real plugin and levelled to the same loudness. Presets that sound wrong are marked, not hidden.

    Download

    A plugin instrument: VST3 for Windows and macOS, plus Audio Unit and CLAP on macOS. Each copy runs its own Windows XP. Open the editor and sixteen real sndrec32.exe windows come apart on XP's desktop and line up into one tape machine, one visible change at a time: menu bar, then title bar, then the buttons they don't need, then the glide into place (about five seconds; it can already play after one). Task Manager's CPU graphs, the clock, four macro sliders and a keyboard of real push buttons follow. Every note you play is played by one of those Sound Recorders, through XP's own sound card, and that sound is what reaches your DAW. Inside each Sound Recorder a small DLL streams its own sound to the card, at the note's pitch, with the note's level, pan and envelope applied.

    The sound is a DNA line, the same format as BASILISK 16. It lists layers of Windows sounds, each run through Sound Recorder's Effects and Edit menus in order. Since 1.2.1 a name means the same file in both editions: ding, chimes, chord and tada are the Windows 3.x recordings, XP's own versions are ding-xp, chimes-xp, chord-xp and tada-xp (written dingxp in a DNA), and the XP startup, shutdown and the rest keep their names (39 in all). Projects saved by 1.2.0 keep their sound. It also sets harmony, unison, transpose and the envelope, and since 1.2 each layer's offset from the note, its pan and a drawn envelope, collages (mix or insert another sound at a position) and four macros you can automate. A library disc in XP's CD drive adds 510 public-domain sounds (instruments, glass and bells, nature, machines, office, water). chord#10/ding>>E#6;A2D300S70R250 is a chord on ten recorders over a sped-up, echoed ding on six. Edit it inside XP (menus, the envelope sliders, the library browser, Mutate) and watch each step being picked from that recorder's own menu, play it from XP's keyboard, use the DAW's parameters, or start from the 43 factory programs (Keys, Bells, Pads, Bass, Plucks, Percussion, Collage, FX, Macro, Brass, Library, Windows 3.x), filtered by [All] [W3.11] [XP] in Windows' browser. Your DAW saves it with the project.

    Install

    WindowsUnzip and copy the BasiliskSynth.vst3 folder to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, then rescan plugins. It is an instrument called BASILISK SYNTH.
    macOSOpen the .dmg and copy BasiliskSynth.vst3 to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3, BasiliskSynth.component to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components, and BasiliskSynth.clap to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP. The AU and CLAP use the machine inside the VST3, so copy the VST3 even if you use only those. They are not notarized, so run this once in Terminal, then restart the DAW: 
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/*/BasiliskSynth.*

    Good to know

    Notes go through a whole emulated PC, and come back about a tenth of a second later. The plugin reports a fixed latency (150 ms on Windows, 100 ms on macOS), which DAWs compensate for on playback; after that, notes land within about 10 ms (Mac) to 40 ms (a busy PC) of the grid rather than on the sample. Offline renders wait for Windows, and for a new sound to be built after a program change, and play it in real time, so they come out like a live bounce. The emulated PC uses about a fifth (Mac) to a half (PC) of one core while it waits with its editor open, about one core while it plays, and next to nothing asleep: it goes to sleep after four quiet seconds with its editor closed and wakes on the next note or when the transport plays. Sixteen voices per instance; a seventeenth note steals the oldest, with a 5 ms fade.

    What is in it

    Windows XP, its sounds and Sound Recorder (© Microsoft), included as part of this art project. The emulator is QEMU (GPLv2), with no window of its own: the plugin draws XP's screen and sends your mouse and keys in. XP's AC97 sound goes into a pipe the plugin reads. Inside XP, a small program assembles the recorders, and a DLL in each Sound Recorder streams its sound to the card with the note's pitch, level, pan and envelope. Both are compiled in XP with tcc. The plugin is built with DPF. Sources are in packages/basilisk-synth.

    Known limits

    • Recording works only inside Sound Recorder: takes cannot be saved as sounds for the synth.
    • Under very heavy load on the host (a parallel compile, a busy WSL2 or Hyper-V), the emulator can starve and notes drop out.
    • On Windows, resuming XP occasionally stalls.
    • XP's timing is not sample-accurate: renders differ a little run to run.
