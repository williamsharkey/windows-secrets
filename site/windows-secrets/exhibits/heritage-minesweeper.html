Heritage · Minesweeper

From OS/2 to a field of flowers

  1. 1989? Curt Johnson writes a mine game for OS/2. Robert Donner ports it to Windows. Both names are still in the 3.11 About box (shown above in the DEBUG dump).
  2. 1990 It ships on 8 October in the Microsoft Entertainment Pack, a box of small games sold alongside Windows 3.0.
  3. 1992 Windows 3.1 includes it in place of Reversi. Like Solitaire's drag and drop, it trains a mouse skill, here the right click. The WfW 3.11 file still calls itself "Minesweeper v3.0a".
  4. 1999 In Italy, Sergio Chiodo starts the "International Campaign to Ban Winmine", saying a game about landmines is offensive. Italian Windows 2000, ME and XP ship it as Prato fiorito, "flower field", with flowers instead of mines and a tune instead of an explosion.
  5. 2001 XP moves its settings from winmine.ini to the registry and loses the Esc boss key.
  6. 2006 Vista's rewrite (Oberon Games) gives everyone a flower-garden option. Windows 8 drops the game, and it moves to the Store.

The 1992 keys, on a running 3.11

The handler shown above checks Menu= in winmine.ini before F5 and F6 do anything. With [Minesweeper] Menu=1 added, the game starts with no menu bar at all verified live:

Esc made the window disappear at once, minimised. That's a boss key, and unlike Wes Cherry's in Solitaire, this one shipped. The Cutting Room Floor already lists Menu=1. I still found no description of Esc as a boss key.

The key handling follows the 3.11 handler at seg1:063A: a five-letter match counter, Shift toggling it between 5 and 17 with xor 14h, F5 and F6 gated on Menu, and Esc posting SC_MINIMIZE. The "pixel" stands in for the real one at the top-left corner of the screen.

Prior art XYZZY, the authors and Prato fiorito are well documented. Menu=1 is in The Cutting Room Floor. The Esc boss key and the Menu-gated F5 and F6 keys were verified live here.
