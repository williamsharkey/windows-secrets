Finding the machine…
 
120 characters, plain words. It types them into XP for you.

Nothing here is a recording. The picture is a Windows XP virtual machine on a server, read straight out of its framebuffer fifteen times a second; the sound is its own sound card. BASILISK is window puppetry: every pixel that moves belongs to Notepad, Paint, Calculator, Minesweeper and the rest, pulled out of their windows by a small program. When the show ends the machine is rewound to the same second of 2001.

One visitor at a time wakes it, and that visitor may type the two questions the console asks. Everyone else watches. The answers come from Claude, told only where its words will appear; when the line is quiet they are old recordings. Nothing from inside Windows reaches your browser except the picture and the sound.

See the films: BASILISK. Questions are filtered and shortened before XP sees them. Be kind to the machine; it is twenty-five years old.
