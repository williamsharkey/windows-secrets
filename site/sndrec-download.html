BASILISK 16 — an instrument made of sixteen Windows 3.11 Sound Recorders

A plugin instrument (VST3 for Windows and macOS, Audio Unit for macOS). Each copy runs its own Windows for Workgroups 3.11. When it loads, sixteen real Sound Recorders lose their menus and title bars and line up into one synthesizer, in under six seconds. Every note you play is played by Sound Recorder: the plugin picks a recorder, Windows presses its Play button, and Sound Recorder's own sound comes out of the emulated sound card into your DAW, at the pitch you played.

The sound is a DNA, a line of Sound Recorder commands run in order: chimesE+;R900 loads CHIMES.WAV, adds an echo, turns it up, and releases over 900 ms. The commands are Sound Recorder's own menus: louder, softer, faster, slower, echo, reverse, copy and paste, insert or mix another sound, delete before or after a position. The DNA also sets harmony, unison, transpose, layers and the envelope. Change any of it in Windows with the panel, from the DAW's parameters, or with the 76 programs. Your DAW saves the DNA with the project.

New in 1.2:

  • Layered sounds. Each layer has its own start offset, pan, and drawn volume and pan envelopes. A seventeenth Sound Recorder draws the selected layer’s envelope in its green wave display.
  • Four macros. They are automatable, and the DNA maps each one onto layer settings. The mod wheel adds to Macro 1.
  • A library disc. 510 public-domain and CC0 sounds on drive L:, now with electromechanical and water sounds, usable by name. Windows 3.1’s CHORD and TADA are back in C:\WINDOWS.
  • A keyboard strip at the bottom, made of Windows 3.11 controls.
    • Five octaves of push-button keys.
    • Bend and Mod scrollbars.
    • Hold, and the computer keyboard (a w s e d f…).
    • Incoming MIDI holds the keys down.
  • Builds you can watch. When a sound is rebuilt, each recorder’s row lights up while it is being built, and its status field names each Sound Recorder command as it runs.
  • 1.2.1:
    • XP’s sounds are on Windows 3.11’s drive, converted for its Sound Recorder: a DNA that names startup or critstop plays XP’s own file, no longer DING. The preset browser in Windows filters [All] [W3.11] [XP].
    • Chord Thump, Critical Bass and Hardware Sub sound on every note.
    • Windows’ screen is drawn pixel for pixel, at 1×, 2× or 3×, never smoothed, on any Windows scaling and on Retina Macs.
    • Bowl Swell is audible.
    • No program peaks above full scale.
    • Macro-driven rebuilds keep every note in offline renders.
    • The silence line in each recorder's wave display is exactly centred.
  • A batch renderer. render311 turns a DNA plus notes into a WAV through the real machine, faster than real time, with identical results on Windows, macOS and Linux.

Install

WindowsUnzip and copy the Basilisk16.vst3 folder to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, then rescan plugins in your DAW. It is an instrument called BASILISK 16. It is not code-signed (there is no signing identity); DAWs load it like any other unsigned plugin.
macOSUnzip. Copy Basilisk16.vst3 to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 and Basilisk16.component to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components. It is ad-hoc signed but not notarized (no Apple developer identity), so run this once in Terminal, then restart the DAW: 
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/Basilisk16.vst3 ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/Basilisk16.component

Good to know

No account and no API key: this one never talks to Claude. The 76 programs are in your DAW’s preset list and in Windows’ Preset drop-down, and you can also type a DNA straight into Windows. A new sound is built while the old one keeps playing: Windows rebuilds one recorder at a time and plays your notes in between, so a preset change never interrupts you. Offline renders wait for the build and come out bit-identical every time, including after reloading the project; live playback never waits (it is silent for the few seconds Windows takes to assemble). It reports a fixed latency of 60 ms, which DAWs compensate for on playback; you hear it when you play live. Each instance is an emulated PC that rests when it has nothing to do: about 13% of one core idle and 35% while playing, on the PC it was built on. Sixteen voices per instance; a seventeenth note steals the oldest.

What is in it

Windows for Workgroups 3.11 and Windows 3.1’s CHORD and TADA (© 1992–1993 Microsoft), included as part of this art project. The emulator is DOSBox Pure (GPLv2 or later), with one addition: BSYN, an emulated 32-voice sound card, because Windows 3.11's Sound Blaster driver can only play one sound at a time. Its Windows 3.11 wave driver, the program that assembles the recorders, and the plugin (built with DPF) are in packages/basilisk-synth-311. The library sounds’ sources and licences are listed on the disc (\LIBRARY.TSV) and in packages/basilisk-synth/library.

BASILISK SYNTH — an instrument made of sixteen Windows XP Sound Recorders

A plugin instrument: VST3 for Windows and macOS, plus Audio Unit and CLAP on macOS. Each copy runs its own Windows XP. Open the editor and sixteen real sndrec32.exe windows come apart on XP's desktop and line up into one tape machine, one visible change at a time: menu bar, then title bar, then the buttons they don't need, then the glide into place (about five seconds; it can already play after one). Task Manager's CPU graphs, the clock, four macro sliders and a keyboard of real push buttons follow. Every note you play is played by one of those Sound Recorders, through XP's own sound card, and that sound is what reaches your DAW. Inside each Sound Recorder a small DLL streams its own sound to the card, at the note's pitch, with the note's level, pan and envelope applied.

The sound is a DNA line, the same format as BASILISK 16. It lists layers of XP's own sounds (ding, chord, tada, the XP startup and shutdown sounds, 35 in all), each run through Sound Recorder's Effects and Edit menus in order. It also sets harmony, unison, transpose and the envelope, and since 1.2 each layer's offset from the note, its pan and a drawn envelope, collages (mix or insert another sound at a position) and four macros you can automate. A library disc in XP's CD drive adds 510 public-domain sounds (instruments, glass and bells, nature, machines, office, water). chord#10/ding>>E#6;A2D300S70R250 is a chord on ten recorders over a sped-up, echoed ding on six. Edit it inside XP (menus, the envelope sliders, the library browser, Mutate) and watch each step being picked from that recorder's own menu, play it from XP's keyboard, use the DAW's parameters, or start from the 39 factory programs (Keys, Bells, Pads, Bass, Plucks, Percussion, Collage, FX, Macro, Brass, Library). Your DAW saves it with the project.

Install

WindowsUnzip and copy the BasiliskSynth.vst3 folder to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, then rescan plugins. It is an instrument called BASILISK SYNTH.
macOSOpen the .dmg and copy BasiliskSynth.vst3 to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3, BasiliskSynth.component to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components, and BasiliskSynth.clap to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP. The AU and CLAP use the machine inside the VST3, so copy the VST3 even if you use only those. They are not notarized, so run this once in Terminal, then restart the DAW: 
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/*/BasiliskSynth.*

Good to know

Notes go through a whole emulated PC, and come back about a tenth of a second later. The plugin reports a fixed latency (100 ms on Windows, 100 ms on macOS), which DAWs compensate for on playback; after that, notes land within about 10 ms (Mac) to 40 ms (a busy PC) of the grid rather than on the sample. Offline renders wait for Windows, and for a new sound to be built after a program change, and play it in real time, so they come out like a live bounce. The emulated PC uses about a fifth (Mac) to a half (PC) of one core while it waits with its editor open, about one core while it plays, and next to nothing asleep: it goes to sleep after four quiet seconds with its editor closed and wakes on the next note or when the transport plays. Sixteen voices per instance; a seventeenth note steals the oldest, with a 5 ms fade.

What is in it

Windows XP, its sounds and Sound Recorder (© Microsoft), included as part of this art project. The emulator is QEMU (GPLv2), with no window of its own: the plugin draws XP's screen and sends your mouse and keys in. XP's AC97 sound goes into a pipe the plugin reads. Inside XP, a small program assembles the recorders, and a DLL in each Sound Recorder streams its sound to the card with the note's pitch, level, pan and envelope. Both are compiled in XP with tcc. The plugin is built with DPF. Sources are in packages/basilisk-synth.
